# Coding protocol

## Unit of evidence

The unit is an exact, minimally sufficient quotation. Preserve enough surrounding text to avoid
changing its meaning. Record a page number, section heading, paragraph index, or media timestamp.

## Claim types

- `policy_support`: supports a concrete policy or institution.
- `policy_opposition`: rejects a concrete policy or institution.
- `value_statement`: states a goal or principle without a policy instrument.
- `candidate_contrast`: directly distinguishes the speaker from another Democrat in the primary.
- `party_contrast`: directly distinguishes the speaker from a political organization.
- `strategy`: describes electoral, legislative, labor, or movement strategy.

## Stance

Use `support`, `oppose`, `mixed`, or `unclear`. Do not infer stance from topic presence.

## Policy comparison

- `agreement`: materially equivalent goal and mechanism.
- `additional_specificity`: one source supplies detail without changing the basic mechanism.
- `stronger_scope`: same direction, but broader eligibility, scale, speed, or institutional reach.
- `different_mechanism`: similar goal pursued through a materially different policy instrument.
- `explicit_disagreement`: a source rejects the other position or the policies are incompatible.
- `insufficient_evidence`: evidence does not support a reliable relationship code.

## Primary sticking point

A topic qualifies as an explicit sticking point only when an official candidate, another Democrat, or
debate source directly frames a disagreement. Analyst-coded divergence is reported separately.

## Reviewer checklist

- Is the speaker correctly identified?
- Is the source official or an allowed archive of official material?
- Does the quote retain necessary qualifications?
- Is the date within the relevant cycle?
- Is the issue and policy-instrument code supported by the text?
- Is an inference labeled as analysis rather than quotation?

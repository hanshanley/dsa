Eon Huntley Spearheads Worker-Power in Bed-Stuy and Crown Heights

Alexandra Chan

Eon Huntley, a lifelong community organizer, union member, and NYC-DSA activist, is running to represent Assembly District 56. In this article, Democratic Left interviews Huntley on his experience as an organizer and activist, bringing his fighting spirit to the New York State Assembly. This interview is one in a series of interviews with members of New York City DSA’s ambitious insurgent slate running in the 2026 election cycle. The Democratic primary is June 23. Early voting runs from June 13 to June 21.

DL: Who are you?

Huntley: My name is Eon Huntley. I am a state candidate for Assembly District 56 in beautiful Bed Stuy and Crown Heights. But I’m also an organizer, former member of the Afrosocialists caucus leadership, still a caucus member of course, a member of the tenant organizing working group, and also a member of New York Communities for Change and obviously a proud member of NYC-DSA. […] Besides being an organizer in that regard, I was a former PTA president at my kids’ school, where I helped to cofound a food pantry and partnerships with Food Bank for New York City. As part of the PTA, we created this program with my friend Nicole Austin, who was the PTA vice president. We did this to serve our community at a time when COVID had just happened, and where our communities were in need of support, and so we stepped up, organized, and created a volunteer base that served the community for several years every third Saturday of the month. 

Also, I’m a retail worker, actually about to go to work right now. And as a retail worker, I worked at Barneys New York. Rest in peace. Barneys New York lives on in our memories and in our closets. It was a place where it was a union shop, and I was part of the bargaining committee and negotiating the best and last contract for the members and fighting, sitting across from the bosses and protecting them against predatory payment plan that they wanted to put us under in terms of like commission, and then also a terrible health care plan under the company, as opposed to what it was under the union. And so I have negotiated and fought for my members as part of the union workplace and democracy, and so, yeah, these are all the things that make up who I am. 

I’m a father of two daughters, a 12-year-old and a nine-year-old, who attend public school. I’m a husband to a wife who is a public school teacher of 20 years, and we really care about New York, and I’m a native who’s lived here his whole life, and I’ve seen people be displaced. I’m originally from Brooklyn. Lived in Brooklyn for the entirety of my life, and it’s a sad situation. So I’ve organized as a member of DSA, but also as someone who stepped up to be a candidate to deliver more for us.

DL: Why are you running?

Huntley: I am someone who, as I explained, in all my life experience and my identity, and someone who’s experienced a lot of things, one as just a millennial who’s witnessed a lot of disappointments of capitalism, that’s further radicalized me. 

My family has created the foundation of who I am. I come from a union family, so the politics that I share are not radical to my family. It’s actually just like preaching to the choir. And so I’ve witnessed the disappointment of the politicians that have come before me, but also witnessed the excitement of what politicians who are rooted in a movement and connected to a movement and actually find their power from a movement by being grassroots candidates, by being people who organize. But then, not just as organizers to fight for action, but also organized in how they run a campaign. And so I am, you know, someone who’s sought to provide and deliver for my community. 

I actually came across Emily Gallagher after going to a school event. And I spoke to her about what it is that I was doing, about what it is that I believed. And she was like, ‘You should run for office. You should run for this assembly seat.’ And she spent a year talking to people in DSA about this, about a DSA member who was living and speaking to the politics that he believes by being involved in the mutual aid program that he helped cofound, and also fighting and advocating for the kind of politics in other ways. So Emily Gallagher, I would say, is a direct reason why I’m running for this seat. But it is always the passion that drives all of us as organizers. And you know, in this instance, I was called and realized that my place is not only just to be an organizer amongst the rest of the masses of people that we are in a part of a movement of but also to be an organizer inside the halls of power, in order to fulfill and deliver on the wins that we’re advocating for on the outside, being organized on the inside.

DL: What’s significant about running a campaign that is endorsed by NYC/DSA?

Huntley: I am an organizer, and it is important to be a member of DSA, a member-led organization where everything that happens happens because of what the members do, what we volunteer to give our time and efforts, energy, and skills and abilities to for something that’s larger than just one individual, larger than us. And so being someone who’s organized within the space and being supported by the same organization means that I’m supported by people just like myself, who have been out there to do what it takes to advance the kind of politics that New York and the masses of people demand, but are not being answered by the establishment and the status quo. So it’s great to be a part of this and be supported by friends and fellow organizers as we carry a message that we are all fighting for, and to be the candidate in this instance amongst the slate of candidates who are all organizers and all part of this effort to do something bigger than us, for all of us.

DL: How will you ensure you adhere to democratic socialist principles both in and out of public view?

Huntley: I’m a person who prides themselves on their integrity. I’m a salesperson, a commission-based salesperson, but I’ve been successful at my job by actually maintaining integrity. And so I think that is also because I’m committed to the principles of what it is that I believe and fight for in all capacities. And so being first and foremost an organizer and someone who’s fighting to bring material change for people like myself and things that I experience currently, with things I have experienced in my life, and also being someone who is put in place and supported by a movement of people that I’m accountable to. This is the thing that keeps the core foundation of who I am. But even beyond that, it’s the people who are in the movement that put me there who will always hold me accountable. And just as easily as we can put people in place, we can also work to take them out. And so I understand that, and I would never betray them or myself for that matter.

DL: Reflect on NYC-DSA’s electoral work in the last five years — what was successful? What were some lessons learned?

Huntley: Yes, I think that we have been great and really moving the overton window and shifting, shifting the politics by delivering, not only like getting people elected like Claire Valdez and Zohran Mamdani, obviously, but although there’s been legislative wins, and I would say even with a campaign like mine in 2024 I think that we did do a lot to make things happen, like getting a version of Good Cause passed because of the pressure. But I think that part of also the thing that we run into that was shown in my first race, and also shown and in Zohran’s race, initially, was the detachment of the Black left community, and some pre existing groups and organizers who are in the fight and believe and support the same things, but [do] not necessarily have the deeper connections, like an organizational connections beyond maybe perhaps individuals within DSA, like Jabari or Phara or myself now, but I think that there’s real effort underway. I know that I’ve been a part of that. Maya, who’s a leader of the Afrosocialists caucus, and others, have been a part of trying to bridge that gap that they saw that was more evident and shown in my race, but also shown initially in Zohran’s, [that] myself and organizers outside DSA people of color work to try to surmount.

DL: Share a favorite eatery/establishment in your district 

Huntley: My favorite pizza joint is Rocco’s Pizza. It can go to either location, but I go to the one more often that’s closer to me, on Halsey. And I also really love Wadadli Jerk, which is on Putnam, and that is another spot that’s excellent jerk, and it’s a vibe. Then there’s a halal chicken spot that’s like, right off the block for me, which they do quite good wings, they rival Wingstop, I would say. Famous Pizza and Burger, or something, is the name of that. Also, shout out to my Deli. That’s right, like they got my sign up, and they receive packages from me, and they also made a lamb and chicken over rice that really slapped, and chopped cheese too. That’s what my daughter gets, the 12-year-old. 

DL: Share a memorable interaction or event that occurred during this campaign.

Huntley: I went out, and I was looking for people on my list, as you do when you canvass. And there was a woman, Miss Ina, whom I ran into; I suspect it was her. She was shoveling snow because at the time when there was snow on the ground. She was someone who was somewhat kind of, like, hostile, to me in the last race, but I took this as an opportunity, like, I don’t ever take anything personal. And I also think about the fact that she just didn’t know me. So that was my opportunity. I’m canvassing, but this is my opportunity, actually, as a candidate, as someone used to a community that really cares about the perspective of their elders, who’ve been fighting for things perhaps, before I was even born. And so I had this great opportunity to talk with her, but also to shovel snow with her. I couldn’t just start chopping it up with her and then just watch her as she shovels snow. It’s like a strong but elderly woman, and so, yeah, I got down in there and did what a decent human being should do and help someone. 

And so, yeah, it was a great opportunity to really just bond and connect, and to hear from her. She has just been around, and I think that part of what I think people feel sometimes is that there’s some inherent view of a kind of disappointment that they expect from some politicians, that’s like big things they expect from a politician, like in terms of, especially, like the financing and money. But, you know, I’m proud to say that we have our state senator and our comrade Jabari Brisport, who has directly proven that you can consistently be on the right side of things and maintain that integrity that we talked about and stay committed to doing the right thing about being on the side of the movement, but also not taking any money from the people that are financing our misery. 

And so she also understood that she voted for Zohran and she voted for Jabari. And you know, I wasn’t necessarily left immediately with the feeling that she was going to vote for me, but I think that she knew that she saw that there was someone who cared about the community and also was committed to doing the right thing, of speaking truth to power, because they don’t compromise themselves in any way. And so she shared the photo of me and her story of us, chopping ice and chopping it up. And, this was something that I think means a lot, that you know, people will see this and know who I was. And it doesn’t necessarily mean that she has to say that she’s gonna vote for me, but she shows that like, ‘Yes, I spoke to Eon, and perhaps you may have personal feelings about a connection you may have with the current elected but know that this is a person that actually truly is committed and fighting for the community and you know, this is a decent person.’ 

I think what we do as organizers, we think about how we build power, and building power is building relationships, building trust, and so like part of that is like having these conversations, and not every conversation you have is going to be where you are speaking to the people that are immediately committed to what it is that you are trying to do, or believe they are actually really on your side. And sometimes it just takes some time for them to get to know you and get to know what it is that you’re about. And perhaps in the end you all find you’re all standing together.

DL: Some thoughts for DSA?

Huntley: I think it’s amazing what we’re accomplishing as an organization, and I think that we need to keep our eye on the prize and continue to mobilize and motivate. You know, we have more eyes on us right now, and I think it’s an opportunity where more people are disappointed in the lack of vision and focus from the Democratic Party, for which we have vision and goals that we’re trying to achieve for the broad majority of people in society and our world. […] So I think that it’s a huge opportunity for us to continue that and to seek every opportunity we can to be involved and the organizing work within DSA, but also outside DSA, like the adjacent things that are happening within your community help to build that trust that is outside of just being a canvasser during an electoral campaign, but actually being a person that’s in community with people, that’s built a relationship so that, you know, there’s true trust around all sorts of things, and true buy in, because you’re invested in the work of building the world that they also care about as well.

Alexandra Chan is a member of New York City DSA and the Democratic Left editorial board.

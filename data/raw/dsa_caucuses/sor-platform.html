Our Platform

Springs of Revolution’s platform builds DSA into a mass revolutionary force that rises to the moment, advances anti-imperialist politics with Palestine as our compass, and fights state repression through abolitionist organizing and immigrant defense. Our organizing projects engage labor, student, immigrant, and liberation movements in broad coalitions with humility and discipline as we build up the community fabric needed to break through imperialist repression and deliver working class victories.

As students and veterans of prior springs of revolution — from the Arab Spring and Occupy Wall Street to the George Floyd uprisings and Student Intifada — we know that our movement grows through ruptures that emerge from the anger of the working class and deep, intentional organizing that identifies, agitates, and escalates on key working class issues. Decisive action taken at the right moment ignites each spring of revolution and expands the political imagination of the working class. We transform ourselves as we transform the world around us.

We are not a caucus. Our cross-tendency team coalesced organically through years of struggle, working together to cut through organizing silos and synthesise a practical, forward-facing revolutionary program. We are planting deep roots in our local communities as willing partners in struggle and building strong chapters bound by shared, coherent national standards that can fight alongside our neighbors all year long.

Revolutionary springs throughout history teach us that momentary ruptures do not, on their own, ready the working class to seize control of their lives and their futures. Without disciplined political organization, even the most ambitious movements buckle under the weight of repression. To endure, we must build resilience. Most of our work happens during the quiet moments between the ruptures: building from below, neighbor to neighbor; together taking on the long-term struggle from which a new world may spring.


FOR A FIGHTING ANTI-ZIONIST DSA (R-22)

This resolution builds on the electoral standards of the Anti-Zionist Resolution introduced by Ahmed H. and passed by the 2023-2025 National Political Committee and over 56 DSA chapters, continuing to provide a material counterweight to the Zionist lobby. Our resolution expands uptake of the Stop Fueling Genocide, No Appetite for Apartheid, and Mask Off Maersk campaigns; establishes an Arms Embargo Steering Committee to increase coordination across labor, legislative, and community organizing efforts to disrupt the flow of weapons to Israel; supports chapters in pursuing divestment through pressure campaigns and ballot initiatives; and develops strategies to protect the movement and build resilience against state repression.

FIGHT FASCIST STATE REPRESSION AND ICE (R-26)

This resolution prioritizes a grassroots approach to resisting fascism and presents three strategic points of activation where DSA can make an incisive intervention: (1) making immigrant defense an organizational priority through a national Abolish ICE campaign; (2) supporting chapters in building local and state-level campaigns around abolitionist demands; (3) scaling up our ability to fight state repression by strengthening Red Rabbits and building infrastructure to support comrades facing repression.

BUILDING A UNITED FRONT TOWARD 2028 (R-37)

Two important political events in 2028 – the presidential elections and the labor movement’s response to the UAW call for a May Day strike – present crucial opportunities for agitational mass organizing. This resolution makes it a priority for DSA to take incremental steps to explore building a broad Left-Labor electoral coalition capable of organizing the foundation of independence from the duopoly, and directs DSA to convene a 2028 Planning Committee to explore the viability of different organizing strategies, liaise across national bodies, and build local chapter preparedness in preparation for 2028.

LOCALS FIRST DSA: INCREASE DUES INCOME FOR LOCALS AND STABILIZE NATIONAL BUDGET (R-43)

Dues share enables our local chapters to pursue ambitious campaigns, maintain equitable membership engagement, and build roots in their communities. This resolution doubles the local chapter’s share of dues to 40% of all dues income by 2027 and thereafter, giving our locals the resources they need to build and sustain new organizing. The resolution also ratifies and increases our national reserves policy to stabilize our national budget, gives chapters the option to receive monthly disbursements, and sets guardrails against cutting dues share in the event of worsening financial health in the organization.


ONE DSA: TOWARD A UNIFIED ENDORSEMENT PROCESS (FORMERLY R-41, NOW CR05-A02)

This amendment abolishes DSA’s current two-tiers endorsement process, which separates the local endorsements conferred by chapters and the national endorsement conferred by the NPC, and replaces it with a unified endorsement process whereby electoral candidates endorsed by their local chapter will be considered endorsed by DSA as a whole. This new process will standardize endorsement criteria across chapters, establish a consistent endorsement revocation process applicable to all endorsed candidates, and make all endorsed candidates eligible to benefit from the full power of DSA’s mass membership and national organization.

DSA for Palestinian Self-Determination (r01-a01)

In being sincere advocates for Palestinian liberation, we must ground ourselves in the grassroots positions of their struggle. This amendment reframes DSA’s commitment to a single Palestinian state as part of a commitment to al-Thawabit, the red lines of the Palestinian people adopted by the Palestinian National Council in 1977, and clarifies that our support for a Palestinian state is the result of our unwavering commitment to their right to self-determination.

FOR A STRIKE-READY LABOR FOR AN ARMS EMBARGO (R42-A01)

This amendment brings the “Labor for an Arms Embargo” resolution further in line with other supply chain-based pressure campaigns already underway in the US and worldwide. This is done by increasing the campaign's emphasis on workers’ self-organization to form international solidarity organs within their unions and to become strike-ready to directly stop the flow of arms. The amendment also removes the mandate to run congressional candidates in the next two years.

Why A Caucus?
Politics is the organization of groups around shared interests. The big tent multi-tendency nature of DSA necessitates the formation of groups around similar interests. This caucus is a political formation of like-minded comrades with the purpose of advancing a political program. The formation of this caucus provides an opportunity to consolidate this program and promote it as the best option within the organization.
We would like this document to serve as an introduction to our politics and priorities in the Champlain Valley and beyond. We believe in the centrality of class struggle and the revolutionary potential of working class self-organization. Our fundamental points of unity are:
1. The competing interests and recurring crises of capitalism create the necessity and possibility of socialism.
2. All workers have common interests opposed to those of capitalists
3. The working class is the primary social force with the interest and power to win democracy and overturn capitalism.
4. The primary task of DSA is to build the fighting capacity of the working class and politicize it in an attempt to integrate socialism into a robust workers’ movement.
5. The working class must take state power, abolish private ownership of the means of production, and establish a worldwide classless society.
6. Democracy is needed for socialists to effectively organize and empower workers to carry out this program.
DSA is and should continue to grow as the leader of the socialist movement in the United States. 
Growing DSA
In Vermont, the Mass Action Caucus is represented across multiple formations, from our comrades at University of Vermont YDSA to Champlain Valley DSA. Here is how we plan to organize inside Champlain Valley DSA.
DSA Toward a Mass Org
We should build DSA as a mass socialist organization - one that can meet the political moment by moving workers into mass action. The ‘left ecosystem’ benefits from DSA’s  growth as the leader of the socialist movement in the United States. DSA is a coherent socialist organization that can assist in leading the charge on political issues. The best way to grow ‘the left’ is to grow DSA and for DSA to be on the frontlines of class struggle. We believe that growing CVDSA and expanding our capacity to do important work is more important than creating broad coalitions with smaller and less ideologically consistent organizations. The political calculus of other groups changes if socialists have enough power to offer a credible alternative to the conservative Democrat/liberal mainstream, even if socialists are not yet hegemonic. Our goal should be to grow DSA as the socialist leadership of all mass movements and build working class power toward revolutionary action against the capitalist class.
Our goals in moving DSA toward a Mass Org
1. Build out DSA into a working class organization
2. Onboard, train, and educate new members to become organizers who can further the socialist movement
3. Increase participation in the front line political, social, and workplace movements of “the left”
4. Further develop DSA as an organization that can lead the charge on movements and campaigns
5. Organize winnable campaigns with achievable goals and develop plans that further the interests of the working class
Centrality of Labor
We believe that socialism can only be achieved through transforming workers’ relationship to institutional and economic power. We believe a strong labor working group can develop the fighting capacity of the working class and politicize it by recruiting the organic leaders of workers’ movement into DSA and directing their organizing toward class struggle. This cannot be accomplished by hawking pamphlets at workers on a picket line; we must earnestly show up in solidarity to aid in the struggle to unite the working class. This support can come in many ways and we support a diversity of tactics as long as they support worker self-organization. As DSA educates, agitates, and assists workers in the struggle for collective power in their workplace, we will improve our visibility and standing among the working class and strengthen our credibility as a vehicle in the fight against capitalism. 
Our goals in the Labor Working Group
1. Organize Workers with Workplace Organizing Trainings and Worker Circles
   1. Initiate new organizing campaigns
   2. Build and support rank-and-file democratic reform movements within existing unions
2. Train DSA members in strike support and other solidarity actions
3. Build a rank-and-file pipeline to recruit DSA Members into:
   1. Long-term careers in strategic sectors such as healthcare, education, and logistics
   2. Short-term salting jobs to support new organizing in traditionally non-union sectors
Democracy is Power
Functional internal democracy is vital to the success of developing DSA into a mass socialist organization. However, democracy should not be considered only in terms of elections but in terms of meaningful participation in discussion and decision-making processes; members should feel genuine ownership over external-facing work we take on. Our task is to grow DSA into the political arm of the working class majority who are not yet politically active. We must create an organizational culture that grows beyond existing activist circles.
We believe that the movement to decentralize the work of our chapter is distracting, and often harmful. The Mass Action Caucus vigorously defends internal chapter democracy, a major part of democratic decision-making is accountability and proper execution. When important chapter work is executed by autonomous, structureless groups, it can undermine the decisions and priorities set by the membership.  If decision-making is dispersed across multiple groups, it becomes inaccessible and confusing. Chapter-wide coordination and integrated work is a precondition to a functional democracy. Structure itself matters and should also be a subject of democratic deliberation.
To grow CVDSA into a healthy mass organization in the next year, we will:
1. Strengthen chapter-wide decision making by creating a culture of democratic accountability.
2. Set standards for committees and working groups that insure their accountability to the priorities set by chapter membership.
3. Set chapter meetings well in advance so members know when to expect them and ensure those meeting agendas have purpose.
   * Our working group meetings should have specific actionable goals such as political education, new member orientations, and campaigns that move workers into mass action.
   * Chapterwide Convention must have stakes and bring consequential decisions to the membership.
4. Develop our executive committee into one that working people can actively and effectively participate in. That means writing and adhering to effective agendas that keep meetings timely and meaningful.
5. Encourage a chapter culture based on mutual respect and healthy boundaries.
   * We believe in treating each other as comrades and not coworkers.
      * We must maintain a standard code of conduct to our members that clearly communicates these boundaries.
   * As an all-volunteer organization, we must always afford each other good faith and grace rather than demand immediate responses or flawless execution of administrative work.
   * Political dialogue, especially when there is disagreement or conflict, should take place through direct conversations and open meetings, in-person as much as possible, rather than entirely through text posts in online forums.
Membership Development
The primary power of the left is people power, especially well-disciplined and experienced organizers. The most urgently necessary way to grow the power of the left is to increase the number of experienced organizers and local leaders.
Some of the most important work that can be done right now includes member recruitment and leadership development. We should focus not only on building out our supporters, but developing supporters into activists and activists into core members. We should heavily emphasize building internal community and solidarity while also forming strong and structured working groups. By shifting our focus from individual events run by loosely affiliated members to prolonged campaigns by a cohesive group of organizers we can become more effective in our purpose and execution.
In order to capitalize on moments, we need strong organizational infrastructure, particularly a robust membership committee to recruit and retain members. Furthermore, CVDSA needs strong communications to publicize our program, inform our supporters, and counter hegemonic capitalist media. We also need to grow our fundraising arm to pay for strategic campaigns, organizing tools, etc.
To develop the membership of CVDSA in the next year, we will:
* Coordinate  regular organizer training to make sure our members are ready to take the lead when the opportunity arises.
* Continue to develop and grow the Membership Committee to manage the onboarding of new members and bring disengaged members back into activity.
* Run regular political education sessions to educate our members on socialist theory and practice.
* Propose the creation of a permanent membership coordinator on the executive committee.
For A Vermont DSA
DSA members are spread across the state of Vermont, some organized and some not. Confusion has arisen multiple times from members who believe Central Vermont DSA and Champlain Valley DSA are the same chapter. Additionally, several missed opportunities have arisen for statewide coordination but a lack of structure failed to meet these opportunities. For these reasons, we support the formation of a statewide Vermont DSA Chapter with the following stipulations:
1. Regional Branches that meet in person regularly.
   1. Jurisdictions big as members can reasonably be expected to travel across weekly.
2. In person statewide semi-annual Conventions to set priorities and elect chapter officers.
3. Statewide Campaign Committees directed toward specific winnable demands widely felt across the state.
   1. e.g. PRO Act, Medicare For All, Just Cause Eviction, etc.
4. Statewide Working Groups tasked with permanent administrative roles.
   1. Communications, Membership Development, Finance, etc.
Democratic Socialism Today
Members of the Mass Action Caucus have been dedicated to the reorganization and development of Champlain Valley DSA. In the next year we plan to orient CVDSA toward the following priorities.
Socialists Should Take State Power
We aim not to destroy or ignore the state, but conquer it. The task of the working class is to become the ruling class. Everything we do must be directed toward this purpose. We have to organize for political power, and short term economic improvements as long as they do not obfuscate this goal. But, material betterment for the working class will require independence. Not just independent messaging but economic and political independence.
Mutual aid is a bandaid, not a solution to our problem. These skills may come in handy in the event of Mad Max-style climate catastrophe. But for now, we still live in an industrial society, in which the capitalist state remains. It’s the duty of socialists to take the state and direct it toward a better world, not to prefigure one through a theater of communalism.
There is no established playbook for how democratic socialists should achieve and maintain power in a liberal democracy. Promising projects in South America have been stymied by American intervention. But in Vermont, we can deploy a diversity of tactics as long as they are part of a coherent strategy.
Rank and File Labor Strategy
Our approach to labor can best be described as one that prioritizes the democratic self-organization of workers. We believe the working class is the driving force for a socialist transformation. Because of this, we prioritize working class organization on the shop floor. Socialists in the US have made many attempts to merge political and labor organizing, but many have failed to organize with workers, instead siding with leadership, staff, and other vestiges of bureaucracy. The Vermont Mass Action Caucus firmly supports a Rank-and-File Strategy that is ready to respond to the new upsurge in workplace organizing.
Labor and Politics
Workers develop class consciousness when they organize collective action against their bosses. This experience is invaluable and irreplicable in our movement today. We do not approach labor organizing with the single goal of calling for a general strike, as others in Vermont have attempted in their labor work. 
We can still reap material benefits from our labor work in the short term. Unions can support our political campaigns. We will not pretend the symbolic approval of union leadership is an indicator of any serious worker power or support. In fact, many unions materially support (donate money to) liberals who actively undermine the workers’ movement. If we want to develop a meaningful alternative to capital-funded democrats, we must find a new financial base to support our work and present our politics as one that aligns with labor. 
New Workplace Organizing, Everywhere
Conditions are ripe for Labor’s revitalization and socialists-as socialists we need to play an instrumental role in driving it, to draw out the contradictions of capitalism. From the unionization drives at Amazon, Starbucks, and beyond, labor is showing signs of movement for new workplace organizing at a pace that hasn’t been seen in decades. Approval for unions is at a 57-year high, or as Starbucks Workers United barista-organizer Jaz Brisack puts it, “Unions are now cool.” But it is evident that socialists must take part in this movement to properly build the fighting capacity of the working class and direct it toward socialism. 
Bureaucratic unions and labor councils have been a hindrance to class struggle unionism in the past and that is still evident. However, instead of narrowly focusing on reforming these already well-entrenched power structures, we have a chance to build new unions from scratch, free from the historical baggage of business unionism. This is not to say we should build only independent unions, but instead that every union we build must be democratic.. Socialists have been able to play a leading role in building locals of even the most corrupt and bureaucratic unions, but our new workplace organizing should not be singularly focused on unionization. Whether through a union drive or issues-based campaign, the goal of new workplace organizing should be to bring workers into direct conflict with the capitalist class. Deeply-felt, widely-felt, winnable issue campaigns develop worker leaders even when they fall short of winning a union election. 
In spite of the stagnation of organized labor in the US since the 1980’s, healthcare unions have remained resilient bastions of workers’ rights. While other unions have waxed and waned, healthcare unions have retained a level of political and economic influence unseen in many other industries. This fact, complemented by new organizing in the face of the COVID-19 pandemic, renders the healthcare industry particularly fruitful for labor organizing. New unions across the country, though their efforts are heartening and deeply important, still remain largely untested in the face of capital retaliation. Healthcare workers and their unions have developed and demonstrated internal structure that is resilient to pressure from the boss. Organizing currently-unrepresented workers and increasing rank-and-file militancy in existing unions are a necessity for anyone who hopes to liberate healthcare from the control of multibillion dollar corporations and strip it of its profit motive.  In addition, these jobs are less vulnerable to being outsourced abroad and less threatened than others by automation. Healthcare workers provide crucial services to the people, especially in a society devoid of any social safety net. We as socialists should do everything in our power as individuals and as members of our communities to advocate for those providing such vital services for the common good.
We should continue to hold workplace organizing trainings and follow up with attendees to form a community of workplace organizers. 
Industrializing Strategic Sectors
While we encourage socialists to organize where they’re at, many of us are indifferent to which workplace to become alienated. We encourage DSA members to take on careers in sectors we deem strategic. A few sectors of the American economy have a strategic value for  organizing as socialists: Education, Healthcare, and Logistics. 
As the US economy moves away from manufacturing, we see logistics, transportation, and the allocation of resources between producers and consumers as a more strategic sector.. DSA can play a role in joining rank-and-file UPS and Amazon workers across the country fighting for the respect they deserve as package handlers.
Teaching and healthcare are integral to their communities and raise the potential for community coalitions with the parents, students, and patients they protect. These sectors offer an opportunity to “bargain for the common good.” When teachers speak up, people listen.  A teachers’ strike creates a kind of political crisis in the city where it happens. If schools are closed in a strike, parents will have to consider new childcare options. Teaching is also attractive because of its recent revival of militancy. Reform movements like the one in the Chicago Teachers’ Union led to the strike wave of 2018–19. Educators also organized around safer working conditions during the COVID-19 pandemic. The Vermont Educators Association and American Federation of Teachers - Vermont are our largest and fastest-growing unions respectively. Encouraging socialists to start careers as educators will pay off in the fight to democratize these unions.
Close cooperation between YDSA and DSA will be imperative for any rank-and-file pipeline as YDSA develops a pool of organizers looking to start careers, and DSA empowers them to organize their workplaces once they join their respective sectors.
Class Struggle Elections
Elections are insufficient for winning socialism by themselves, but they are a powerful tool. Good campaigns grow and develop our membership toward winnable demands. Canvassing in our communities brings us in direct contact with working-class voters. We cannot rely on outside pressure; we need to take the state and support a mass movement for socialist transformation. 
To win at the ballot box, we must build our electoral working group and chart a clear path toward building an independent workers’ party. 
Progressive Party-Surrogate
Vermont’s Champlain Valley is unique in the United States in having a viable third party (the Progressive Party)  in addition to the Democratic and Republican Parties. Instead of running local campaigns exclusively for Democratic primaries, we can and do run our candidates as Progressives or fusion Progressive-Democrats. 
The Progressive Party got its start as a merger of a loose coalition surrounding the independent Mayor Bernie Sanders. Today, this coalition, along with the state’s robust ecosystem of nonprofits, continues to offer a viable alternative to the Democrats. The Progressive base mainly comprises college students and retired hippies, not working people. While the Progressive platform is not explicitly socialist, it gestures in multiple directions, including calls for a “democratic economy.” The Progressive Party is also the political home of all of CVDSA’s endorsed electeds.
Unlike the Democratic and Republican Parties, the Progressive Party’s financial base is mostly small donors, not capital. Because of this, there is a clear possibility for party realignment.
Worker Party
Even for a CVDSA member whose ideology may sit slightly less comfortably alongside that of the Progressive Party leadership, winning a nomination for City Council or State House would not necessarily involve a “hostile takeover.” By developing stronger ties to local unions and recruiting labor organizers to run for office as Progressives on a DSA platform, we can demonstrate the possibility of a new base for left-wing politics in Vermont and contribute to a party realignment. The Progressives already possess a sympathetic connection to the working class; it can be our role to develop that connection further with our continued rank-and-file strategy.
Contesting Primaries
While we build our base in labor and issue campaigns,  we can align the Progressives to socialism by running CVDSA Members for office on the Progressive ballot line. This means showing up for nominating caucuses and running CVDSA members for internal party leadership. We must extend our effort to recruit internally for potential candidates. The consequence of finding members internally is an important one, because it means that those candidates will be accountable to CVDSA and our members above special interests and lobbyists while in office. 
While Democratic Party realignment has been a failed endeavor for DSA members for some time now, a true party surrogate can move its base to other ballot lines.  With the Progressive Party’s sluggishness to grow independence outside of Burlington’s city council, it has become evident that we will need to contest Democratic Party primaries as well. This contest with liberals will be far less civil than our internal matters with the Progressives. In these campaigns we should aim to build more political independence from Democrats.
Political Independence
Lasting political independence will come from patient base-building and class formation, mostly through our labor organizing. In this more propagandistic stage, we believe that socialists in office should publicly state their belief in democratic socialism and include democratic socialism on their campaign material. This will not alienate our electorate; it will involve them in our movement. Furthermore, DSA candidates should downplay their affiliation with the Democratic Party if they choose to run as a fusion candidate.
Socialists seeking elected office should endorse our other endorsed candidates, and publicly identify themselves as a slate when appropriate. Furthermore, candidates will be asked, if they and other DSA members are elected to the same body, to form a socialist caucus and vote as a unified bloc. 
We will still continue to recommend the most progressive candidates in any race our membership will vote in. But due to our limited capacity and vision for an independent workers party we should only materially support campaigns that will grow our organization and align with our values. Our goal is to make CVDSA more Nonpartisan League and less Justice Democrats.  
Candidate Pipeline
The most successful socialist electoral campaigns across the country often come from working in coalition with other organizations and community groups, especially organized labor. But as socialists, we have our own analysis and program. We don’t just want to get behind charismatic leaders, we want to develop disciplined candidates who we know will be accountable to our members and their communities. This is how we turn the organic leaders of mass movements into the elected leaders of a workers’ government. While we continue to develop the mass organization necessary to make our interests practically unignorable, we can grow our pool of viable socialist cadre ready to carry out DSA’s platform in office. These organizers can come from student governments, labor leadership, or grassroots movements.
Socialists in Office
We should keep a relationship with our electeds throughout the year, not just election season. It is imperative that we create a formation for the chapter and our electeds to strategize across offices.
Resolutions
Protect The Right To Organize In Vermont
Decades of anti-labor legislation have hindered the workers’ movement. Despite this, we are witnessing a historic upsurge in new workplace organizing from Starbucks, to Amazon, and beyond. Many of these union drives have greatly benefited from the recent rulings of the National Labor Relations Board, but socialists should demand more. 
Legislation will be introduced in the next session of the state legislature, demanding the following:
1. Ending at-will employment and requiring just-cause job protections
2. Collective bargaining rights for domestic and agricultural workers
3. Ending captive audience meetings
4. Majority Sign Up to form a union (Card check)
Be It Resolved:
Champlain Valley DSA will start a campaign to Protect The Right To Organize. This campaign will be run in collaboration with Central Vermont DSA and the Vermont State Labor Council.
Vermont Statewide DSA
Whereas:
DSA members are spread across the state of Vermont, some organized and some not. Confusion has arisen multiple times from less savvy members who believe Central Vermont DSA and Champlain Valley DSA are the same chapter. We are not. Additionally, several missed opportunities have arisen for statewide coordination but a lack of structure failed to meet these opportunities. The organizational division between Central Vermont  DSA and Champlain Valley DSA has created clear redundancies and deficiencies in our shared goals of realizing Democratic Socialism in Vermont. Collaboration between University of Vermont YDSA and Champlain Valley DSA has yielded incredible results in developing leaders and growing capacity in collaborative campaigns.
Resolved:
Champlain Valley DSA will lead an initiative to form a Single Chapter of the Democratic Socialists of America covering all of Vermont. This initiative will include a merger of Champlain Valley DSA, Central Vermont DSA, Upper Valley DSA, and University of Vermont DSA as well as an expansion to regions of Southern Vermont that have not been organized as DSA yet.
We set the following goals for a Vermont DSA:
1. Regional Branches that meet in person regularly
   * While Vermont is one of the smallest states in the country, it is still too vast to expect members to commute regularly to meetings.
   * We will divide the chapter into smaller branches so members can reasonably be expected to travel across weekly.
      * These branches could include the following counties
         1. Champlain Valley
            * Chittden, Grand Isle, Franklin, Franklin Counties
         2. Central Vermont
            * Washington, Orange, Lamoille
         3. Southern Vermont
            * Rutland, Bennington
         4. Upper Valley
            * Windsor, Windham
         5. NorthEast Kingdom
            * Essex, Orleans, Caledonia
2. In person statewide semi-annual Conventions to set priorities and elect chapter officers.
   * If the officers of the chapter cannot find any venue that can host every member in good standing, we will consider a representative delegate system
3. Statewide Campaign Committees directed toward specific winnable demands widely felt across the state.
   * PRO-ACT, Medicare For All, Reproductive Liberty Amendment, etc.
4. Statewide Working Groups tasked with permanent administrative roles
   * Comms, Membership Development, Finance, etc

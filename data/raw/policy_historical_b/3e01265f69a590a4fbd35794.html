Oregon House

  • District 7th District: Springfield
  • Term Length 2 years
  • Salary $46,308, plus $178 daily allowance during session
  • Topics MOTIVATION, COMMITTEES, GOALS, TRANSPORTATION, DISTRICT ISSUES, FEDERALISM, FIRE, COST OF LIVING, DECISION-MAKING, FUNDRAISING

The retirement of Rep. John Lively, D-Springfield, opened the Democratic-leaning 7th House District. The winner of a three-way Democratic primary will face Republican Adam Wilson, a  wealth adviser running in an uncontested GOP primary, in November.

Nonaffiliated voters make up 42% of the district, followed by Democrats at 32% and Republicans at 23%.

The Candidates

Ky Fireside

Democrat

Ky Fireside, an archaeologist, led unionization efforts at a Starbucks and worked on legislation on license-plate reading technology before running for office.

KC Huffman

Democrat

KC Huffman is an attorney and high school baseball coach.

Democrat

Kori Rodley is a Springfield city councilor and works for Lane County Developmental Disabilities Services.

MOTIVATION

Why are you running?

Ky Fireside (D)

I’m running because our current politicians have rubber-stamped us into chaos and crisis. They’ve caved to the lobbying interests of giant, private corporations and we, the working class, are left fighting over crumbs. As a community organizer, I’ve already fought multi-billion dollar companies in the legislature, and won. I’m going to bring that fight to the inside now.

KC Huffman (D)

I believe service to the community is how we make life better. I admire what John Lively has done and would like to continue with his good work.

Kori Rodley (D)

Having served as a city councilor (and in my second term), I am passionate about improving lives for Springfield residents. We have worked hard at the local level to increase housing, improve accessibility and livability in our communities, address difficult workforce issues (Eugene Springfield Fire Governance), and attracting and directing funding for necessary services. There is still so much work to do and I have learned how the Legislature can both help and hurt these efforts. Not only do I have a deep understanding and love of Springfield, I also understand government at all levels and what it takes to make real change happen. There is much to do, no one single issue is impacting Springfield residents, and I believe it will take experience and steady commitment to get real work done in Salem.

COMMITTEES

Which committees do you most want to serve on?

Ky Fireside (D)

I want to serve on the Commerce and Consumer Protection and Information Management and Technology Committees, so that I can work on protecting Oregonians from the harms those who put profits over people. No monopolies. Keep Oregonians’ private data private. Ensure right-to-repair.

KC Huffman (D)

I don't have a strong preference. I would like to be involved in education, judicial selection, and auditing of existing programs to identify inefficiencies.

Kori Rodley (D)

  • Early Childhood & Human Services
  • Labor & Workforce Development
  • Behavioral Health
  • Public Education Appropriation (Joint)
  • Ways & Means sub-committee on Human Services

GOALS

What three goals you would like to accomplish if elected? How would you gather support?

Ky Fireside (D)

Oregon can and will have universal healthcare. This fall, we’ll receive a proposal detailing how Oregon can transition to universal healthcare. The legislature will vote on it in 2027. There is already widespread support, but my organizing experiences taught me how to educate people from different backgrounds on complex issues. Educating our electeds and our community will help ensure that we have the votes needed.

Get private equity out of single-family home ownership and pass a Renters’ Bill of Rights. Getting affordable housing options back will take require us to come at the problem from many angles, but these two goals are a great start. Private equity has been driving up housing costs through unfair market manipulation tactics. People need homes, private equity doesn’t. The Renters’ Bill of Rights will ensure many of the basic protections renters deserve, but currently lack. It would also implement stricter price caps.

The state’s budget needs to be rebalanced because our priorities are out of order. We need to tax big business while funding education and healthcare first. The wealth gap between the wealthiest and the working class is unacceptable. This reform will be some of the most difficult to pass because lobby groups will fight it the hardest. However, even our lawmakers are working class people, and many have struggled financially at one point in their lives. Finding common ground in the shared experience of those difficulties will be essential.

KC Huffman (D)

I would like to get the infrastructure issue resolved. I would like to continue the work done on universal health care. I would like to further investigate childcare options. Gathering support is at least a two-pronged approach. First, come up with a good idea and plan for your proposal and second, talk with other members of the legislature to gather support and learn about opposing views. Collaboration is critical to good governing.

Kori Rodley (D)

  1. Increased flexibility in meeting Climate Friendly & Equitable Cities and middle housing goals so they would be more compatible with small municipalities like Springfield
  2. Stronger worker and workplace safety laws
  3. Increased affordable childcare throughout Oregon, particularly in underserved communities

TRANSPORTATION

After the Legislature failed to pass a sustainable long-term transportation funding plan in 2025 or 2026, transportation funding is expected to be a major issue again in 2027. What would you prioritize? Where would you find the money to pay for it?

Ky Fireside (D)

This problem has been around for decades and can’t be solved in 250 words. I want to make sure any plan we have for increased revenue isn’t relying on further burdening the working class with increased taxes. Fuel is already expensive and most of us are just driving to work. My priority will always be advocating for the good of the working class. The solution here is to look at ODOT’s budget. We need an audit completed by forensic accountants to find where this money is really going. We overpay for contractors when those are jobs that could be done in house. Instead of adding more taxes, we need to make sure we’re responsibly spending the money we have.

KC Huffman (D)

We have to keep our roads and bridges in good shape because our economy relies on it. We have to find a way to share the load, no pun intended, and that means considering gas tax, registration fees and travel tax as part of the solution. Those who use the roads more should bear more of the responsibility to keep them in good condition. Your taxes are too high? So are mine. The problem is this is critical infrastructure so we can't just say "no."

Kori Rodley (D)

I lobbied on behalf of the City of Springfield in support of this package and will share why, along with what I viewed as short-comings: This package provided much-needed funding for infrastructure right here in Springfield. It is very difficult to fund maintenance and improvements (like repairing roads and fixing potholes) for cities like Springfield and these are issues residents value highly.

I agreed there was not adequate accountability and I also felt like this bill didn’t do enough to help tackle transportation challenges, but it was a start. I understand people don’t want to pay more taxes AND we have a real revenue problem in Oregon. This bill would have provided much-needed funding for transportation projects throughout Oregon.

This experience should guide us in looking at who is most impacted by added taxes or fees and making sure we minimize impacts on people who have the least. We have the opportunity to come back and work on this to get it right.

DISTRICT ISSUES

What issue in your district hasn’t been adequately addressed by the Legislature? How would you address it?

Ky Fireside (D)

ICE has wreaked havoc on our community. Apartments have been raided. People have been kidnapped. The legislature tried to pass some legislation during this most recent session, but much of it was watered down. I support requirements for identification and any other ICE restrictions. I want to focus on mass surveillance. Much of the ICE machine is powered by surveillance data they collect on people’s locations and movements. We need a crackdown on the technologies that ICE is using in their attacks on our community.

KC Huffman (D)

Springfield's education system is hurting. Teachers I've talked with feel they are restricted in what they can do in the classroom. Schools are the foundation for our communities, so we need to re-examine how we're doing things. We don't have to re-invent the wheel because there are districts where schools are doing well and we can learn from them. We need to be organized in how we approach the what and how we are teaching and consider the input of the teachers.

Kori Rodley (D)

We have a precarious levee in Springfield known as the 42nd Street levee. If this levee were to fail, much of Springfield would be flooded. I know that we are not alone, and the flooding in Burns is a perfect example of what happens when we do not address these strained and failing older infrastructure projects. We have been working to accumulate funding for years and have patched together enough to do a feasibility study AND federal funding has been precarious at best in the past couple years. I believe we need to prioritize these older maintenance issues throughout the state and prioritize people’s safety by directing resources for repairs and improvements.

FEDERALISM

How do you believe state government should interact with the federal government, especially on issues like immigration, reproductive rights and climate?

Ky Fireside (D)

The state’s responsibility is to its people. That means protecting its people from the overreach of the federal government. We are a Sanctuary state and we should strengthen those protections. The same goes for Shield Law. Reproductive choices are individual and medical and the state should not try to legislate such a nuanced topic as abortion. The climate needs to be protected and it is the state’s responsibility to regulate polluters and move to clean, renewable energy. As the federal government terminates regulatory agencies and targets vulnerable groups, the state needs to step up for its people.

KC Huffman (D)

We are the best stewards of our state and we should continue to lead in the way we, in Oregon, feel is best. There are some national issues which require national solutions, but the 10th amendment reserves to the states all that the federal government has not pre-empted. Oregon has been on the cutting edge of a lot of movements (bottle bill, death with dignity, public access to beaches, etc.) and we should continue to govern with the same mindset that has served us well.

Kori Rodley (D)

Addressing immigration, reproductive/healthcare rights, and climate impacts work best when we are in partnership with the federal government. That is definitely not the case right now. Oregonians value our immigrant neighbors and are overwhelmingly in support of access to reproductive care — since the federal government is in opposition to these values, it is helpful for us to partner and collaborate with other states and invest in partnerships with other organizations and entities. Addressing climate on the state level is important AND we can only do so much region by region. During times like these, our Attorney General is incredibly important in helping us to use the courts to defend and advance legislation and it is increasingly important for us to codify these goals in legislation that is defendable against federal overreach.

FIRE

What would you do in the Legislature to address fire prevention and rebuilding after fire or other natural disasters?

Ky Fireside (D)

Most of my professional work is protecting cultural resources during fire mitigation efforts and post-fire cleanup work. Fire prevention is complicated and we need to involve and listen to the subject-matter experts. Protection of old-growth forest is one important step we can take to mitigate fire damage. Regardless of what entity is responsible for the fire or disaster, the first priority is for people affected to be made right.

KC Huffman (D)

I support active forest management. I am also in favor of conservation of historic timberlands. There is a middle ground and that is where I would like to see us get to. Our forests are capable of being renewable resources and pristine wonderlands for the public to explore and enjoy.

Kori Rodley (D)

A few years ago, we experienced the Holiday Farm Fire here in Springfield and we are still rebuilding and addressing the impacts. It takes so long to get funding and reimbursement from the federal government, I think we need a reserve fund here in Oregon that can be accessed when needed.

I know there is conversation and work to designate part of the “kicker” funds for wildfire response and I support that. The more prepared we are, the better — this means investing in education and wildfire preparation work in our communities and making sure residents have what they need to build resiliency on the local level.

Supporting and providing resources for localized emergency response and planning will also go a long way in making sure we can respond quickly when wildfires happen. We also cannot ignore the need for forest management — controlled burns, clearing up dry and dangerous areas and ensuring we invest in the workforce to do this important resiliency building.

COST OF LIVING

What would you do in the Legislature to reduce cost of living? 

Ky Fireside (D)

I will work on housing prices, as stated above. I will address price-fixing and monopolies at grocery stores which are driving up food costs. I am also staunchly against surge pricing and surveillance pricing in all retail.

KC Huffman (D)

There are so many components that go into the cost of living, many of which are not in the control of the Oregon Legislature. I would first want to make sure we're focusing on the things we can control rather than waste time on something we won't be able to affect.

I think universal health care will ultimately help reduce the cost of living. Right now, people without adequate health care are either not getting the help they need, which leads to more severe health care issues, or are using the emergency departments as their primary health care provider. This causes stress on the system, which raises costs. To the extent we can create an orderly way of handling health care so people know what is available to them and providers and insurance companies can forecast their costs with some amount of certainty, we will reduce the cost of living.

Kori Rodley (D)

I believe affordable, stable housing is at the root of reducing the cost of living for Oregonians. Tools such as rent relief and support, increasing the availability of affordable and workforce housing and working to keep utility rates down all help the average person with affordability. Tax credits and rebates for low income families, along with incentivizing and supporting small businesses in our communities, also help to keep costs down and improve livability.

Universal, affordable healthcare is important work we are moving through policy-making tables in Oregon, and I believe we could be the first state to implement healthcare for all Oregonians. Investing in pubic education, workforce training, apprenticeship programs and supporting unions also help to strengthen the wealth and stability of people through the state.

DECISION-MAKING

How would you decide how to vote on each bill that comes before you?

Ky Fireside (D)

Every bill deserves careful consideration of all stakeholders. I will listen to my constituents and subject-matter experts. My votes will be for humanity and for the greater benefit of all of us.

KC Huffman (D)

The same way I approach personal decisions in my life. 1. Get the facts; 2. Consider other viewpoints; and 3. Make the decision that provides the best outcome.

Kori Rodley (D)

I am a collaborative problem-solver who likes to take time to see “the whole elephant.” It will be important for me to first represent the constituents of House District 7, then consider impacts and consult subject matter experts and those who are most impacted by the potential legislation. I will consider financial implications and the position of the caucus, as well as looking ahead to how potential legislation will potentially help or harm in the future.

One of the lenses I’ve brought to decision-making at the city council level is to ask myself what the possible impact will be 10-20 years into the future. Of course values come into play, but I have made it a practice not to dig in on principle when it comes to policy work.

FUNDRAISING

How much has each candidate raised for their campaign?

Ky Fireside (D)

Fireside had raised more than $17,000 as of April 29, with $8,000 remaining in their account.

KC Huffman (D)

Huffman has not reported any fundraising or spending.

Kori Rodley (D)

The race for Erie City Council: Democrat Maurice Troop brings passion for service

Kevin Flowers
Erie Times-News
View Comments

On Maurice “Mo” Troop’s Facebook campaign page, the objectives of his candidacy for Erie City Council are clearly laid out.

Troop, a 45-year-old assistant principal Erie High School, states that he wants to “serve the entire community, meet the needs of the people and help create a better future for the city.”  

He is one of eight Democrats and two Republicans seeking one of the four City Council seats on the ballot in the May 18 municipal primary.

City Council members serve part-time and are paid $6,000 a year; the council’s president is paid $7,500 annually.  Council members are also eligible for health insurance benefits.

The Erie Times-News asked each of the 10 candidates a series of questions about the City Council race. Here are Troop’s responses to some of those questions. 

What unique ability or skill would you bring to City Council?

The unique skill that I would bring to council, is the leadership ability to inspire a diverse group of talented individuals to work together to set and achieve common goals that benefit the people of Erie.

What should city government do right now to control expenses and/or create new revenues?

City government could do a thorough analysis of all current spending. After the analysis, the city could prioritize the spending. It would also be great to listen to the Erie citizens, business owners, etc. to find out how they feel spending should be prioritized. 

After prioritizing and assessing the current spending, city government could decide where to add, cease, or create new revenues. If necessary, an outside agency that specializes in helping cities optimize spending could be brought in to help.

In what specific way would you like to see city’s nearly $80 million in American Rescue Plan funding utilized?  

When it comes to the $80 million Erie has received from the American Rescue Plan fund, it would first be great to hear from the Erie Citizens to get their thoughts on how they feel the funding should be utilized. I feel that it could be utilized to:

  • Develop and enhance opportunities for all Erie citizens ranging from youth to senior residents.
  • Fund existing or create organizations and programs that assist the homeless population and unemployed.
  • Fund existing and new initiatives that deal with building a safe community.
  • Creating vibrant and attractive neighborhoods in all parts of the city.

A majority of current City Council members have said they favor creating a civilian review board to examine claims of police misconduct. Do you support such a move? Why/why not?

I do support a civilian review board. Police are important and are a big part of keeping a community safe. A civilian review board would decrease the feeling in the community that misconduct is being swept under the carpet and increase transparency. This review board could help to formulate data that helps to make clear areas of strength and areas of needed improvement.

Have you read the Erie Refocused comprehensive plan? What component(s) of the plan should the city prioritize, and is there any aspect of the plan you disagree with?

In terms of the Erie Refocused Comprehensive Plan, it is great that the plan was created with the mindset of it being a flexible plan. This flexibility allows for adjustment of the plan based on the current conditions and circumstances existing in the city at any given moment in the future.

Every corner of the city is important and has value. This should be remembered when decisions are made. New flower beds on the upper west side should also mean new flower beds on the lower east side.

If you had an initiative you want to move forward as a member of City Council, explain how you would successfully create the support needed among fellow council members and/or the mayor’s administration to make it happen.

To move an initiative forward, I would:

  • Check to see if there is an existing initiative that is similar to the initiative, I desire to gain support for.
  • Gather data about an existing need or lack that needs to be addressed in the city.
  • Develop a presentation for council.
  • Show what it is, why it is needed, the benefits, plan for implementation, timeline for implementation, etc.
  • It would also be great to show cities where similar initiatives are benefiting other cities positively.
  • I would be open to suggestions from council and the mayor that could improve the initiative.

About the candidate

  • Age: 45
  • Occupation: Assistant principal at Erie High School
  • Education: Bachelor’s degree from Westminster College; Master’s degree from Edinboro University
  • Personal: Married with three children

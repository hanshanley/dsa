"I believe local government can be more democratic, ambitious, and responsive to people’s actual needs, and that’s the kind of city I want to help build." - AMY RONDEL

Hi, my name is Amy Rondel, and I'm running for City Council in Ypsilanti's Ward 2.

I have lived in southeast Michigan for most of my life, first in Ann Arbor and now in Ypsilanti. I love it here. I love the river and the trees, the historic neighborhoods and corner stores, the festivals and farmers markets. But more than anything, I love the people who make this city what it is: the neighbors who shovel each other's sidewalks, organize block parties, volunteer at local nonprofits, and show up when someone needs help. Ypsilanti is a place where people still believe that community means something.

Like many people in our city, my path here hasn't been straightforward. I've worked in the food industry, with nonprofits, in retail, technical support and sold building materials. I've organized mutual aid efforts, advocated for vulnerable communities, and helped build organizations from the ground up. Through all of it, I've learned that the strongest communities aren't built by waiting for someone else to solve our problems. They're built by ordinary people who decide to step up, listen to one another, and get to work.

But here's the thing: Every generation inherits a city shaped by the choices of those who came before. Our generation has the responsibility to decide what we leave behind.

We can cling to systems that aren't meeting people's needs, or we can build something better. We can invest in the things that make a community strong: affordable housing, reliable infrastructure, thriving public spaces, and neighbors who know they can count on one another.

I believe Ypsilanti is uniquely suited to meet this moment. We are a city of people who show up for each other. We are creative, resilient, and unafraid to do things differently. We don't have to settle for managing decline or reacting to one crisis after another. We can choose to build the kind of city we want to become.

For me, that means protecting and improving the things we share: our parks and public spaces, our infrastructure, our natural resources, and the institutions that bind us together. It means making sure that the people who work here can afford to live here. It means planning not just for the next budget cycle, but for the next generation.

I love Ypsilanti not because it's perfect, but because of what it makes possible. Together, we can build a city that is affordable, welcoming, sustainable, and ready for whatever the future brings.

Amy Rondel

THE ISSUES I CARE ABOUT MOST

Stronger Neighborhoods

Our neighborhoods should make daily life easier, not harder.

I support zoning and planning policies that allow small, neighborhood-scale businesses, more flexible housing options, and walkable communities where people can get what they need close to home. Growth should happen in ways that strengthen neighborhoods, expand opportunity, and preserve what residents value about the places they live.

Local Control & Accountable Services

When we spend public money, it should deliver real results.

I will support reviewing outsourced contracts to identify where bringing services back under local control can improve quality, increase accountability, and reduce long-term costs. Public investment should also create dignified jobs that allow the people who serve our community to live here comfortably. Residents deserve services they can rely on and a government that stands behind the work it performs.

Helping Residents Stay and Thrive

Strong communities are built when people have the support they need to navigate life's challenges.

I support expanding city programs that connect residents with resources, address small problems before they become major crises, and help longtime residents age in place with dignity and independence. Government should be a partner in helping people build stable, connected lives.

Public Space That Serves the Public

Public space should actually work for the community.

I support reviewing how we use public land and identifying opportunities to expand parks, strengthen neighborhood gathering places, support small businesses, and create spaces where people want to spend time together. Our public assets are part of a shared inheritance. We have a responsibility to steward them wisely and ensure they serve the people of Ypsilanti today and for generations to come.

Transparent, Responsive Government

Residents deserve a city government that listens, communicates clearly, and earns trust through its actions.

I support improving communication around city decisions, expanding opportunities for meaningful public participation, and making it easier for residents to understand how decisions are made and how to engage with them. Good government is not just about making decisions. It is about ensuring people understand the process and know their voices matter.

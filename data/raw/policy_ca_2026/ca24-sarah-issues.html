Fight Corruption & Fix Congress
Big money has led to concentrated power, and concentrated power has corrupted our government. Nothing else works until we fix that.
Signature Position

How do we fix who gets elected?

Politicians shouldn't get to choose their voters. Right now they do, through gerrymandering, through winner-take-all districts, through a system designed to entrench whoever is already in power. The result is a Congress that doesn't actually represent the American people.

Most campaigns operate at the level of policy promises. But institutions determine outcomes more than policies do. If we don't fix the structure, every policy fight is uphill. That's why structural reform is my top priority, because nothing else works without it.

I support the Fair Representation Act (H.R. 4632), introduced by Don Beyer and co-sponsored by Jamie Raskin, Ro Khanna, Jim McGovern, Scott Peters, Joe Neguse, and Rashida Tlaib. The Fair Representation Act would change how we elect members of Congress:

  • Ranked-choice voting: Improve elections by allowing voters to rank candidates in order of preference.
  • Multi-member districts: Larger districts that elect multiple representatives, which ensures voters have better representation.
  • Independent redistricting: Nonpartisan commissions draw maps, not politicians.

Term limits and term lengths: Term limits and term structure matter, but the evidence is more complex than the talking points suggest. I'm evaluating the data carefully and will take a position based on what actually improves representation and governance. I'm considering term limits, such as a maximum of 6 two-year terms, and longer terms such as moving House members to 4-year staggered terms so members can govern rather than campaign from Day One.

Expanding the House: The Constitution set no fixed size for the House of Representatives. We've been stuck at 435 members since 1929, while the population has tripled. Each member now represents nearly 800,000 people. That's not representation.

Signature Position

How do we fix how Congress operates?

Democracy only works when institutions answer to the people. Right now, they unfortunately do not. Decades of concentrated money have consolidated power, and Congress has surrendered much of its authority to the executive branch rather than fight to take it back.

A congressperson's real leverage is behavior, not legislation. I will refuse corporate PAC money, demand oversight hearings, and vote against continuing resolutions that fund executive overreach.

I'll strategically use PR as part of a diversity of tactics to raise awareness and get the word out about what Congress is doing and where help from the people is needed. I'll publicly call out performative legislation, including from members of my own party, when it's designed to generate headlines rather than change. Press conferences and press releases are not an outcome on their own.

Reforming how Congress works:

  • Require all committee votes to be public record.
  • Limit proxy voting to defined emergency circumstances with full public transparency.
  • Rebuild the Office of Technology Assessment so Congress has independent in-house expertise and isn't dependent on industry lobbyists to understand the technologies it's supposed to regulate.

Asserting Congress's constitutional role:

  • Use committee assignments to demand real oversight hearings. Not grandstanding or name-calling or feel-good speeches. Actual accountability.
  • Oppose any legislation that hands power from Congress to the executive branch.
  • Require Congress to set clear standards when delegating to federal agencies. Decades of blank-check delegations have handed away congressional power conferred by Article I of the Constitution.
  • Restore war powers to Congress. The Constitution is clear that Congress declares war, not the president, and I will vote against any military action taken without congressional authorization.
  • Vote against any continuing resolution that funds programs Congress has explicitly rejected. The power of the purse belongs to Congress.
  • Reform the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) to end presidential abuse of emergency economic powers. No president should be able to unilaterally impose tariffs or sanctions without congressional approval, as the Constitution gives Congress authority over trade.
  • Reform the Congressional Review Act. It has been weaponized to gut regulations with a simple majority vote and it bypasses the legislative process it was designed to protect.
Signature Position

How do we fix who Congress works for?

Members of Congress spend hours every day raising money from political insiders and corporate PACs. Too often, these political contributions are aligned with the committees that a representative is assigned to.

When politicians depend on big donors to stay in office, the system starts working for those donors and not for the people they represent.

This campaign accepts no corporate PAC money, no oligarch money, no AIPAC money, no weapons manufacturer money, no fossil fuel money, no crypto or AI donations. Every donation is publicly reported to the FEC.

Getting money out of politics:

  • Ban congressional stock trading and support the Ending Trading and Holdings in Congressional Stocks (ETHICS) Act (H.R. 4890). Public office should not be used for personal gain.
  • Require blind trusts for all members of Congress and their spouses. Ensure that decisions are made in the public interest and not influenced by personal investments.
  • Support the DISCLOSE Act to require full transparency in political spending.
  • Create public financing for elections. Campaigns should be powered by people and not dependent on wealthy donors. New York City, Los Angeles, Seattle, Arizona, and Maine already have versions of this.
  • Overturn Citizens United. Unlimited corporate political spending has distorted our democracy. It needs to end.
  • Strengthen and enforce ethics and disclosure rules. Laws don't matter if they aren't enforced. Congress must be held to a higher standard.
  • Protect whistleblowers. People who expose corruption and abuse should be protected, not punished.

Closing the revolving door:

  • Support the Close the Revolving Door Act (H.R. 3554), a lifetime ban on former members of Congress becoming lobbyists.
  • Require full disclosure of all lobbyist contacts with congressional offices.
  • Restrict former lobbyists from taking senior congressional staff or regulatory positions; the revolving door spins both ways.

  • Invest in federally qualified health centers and public clinics in underserved areas of CA-24.

Defend Rights & Human Dignity
Everyone deserves to live on their own terms, free from government overreach and corporate manipulation.
How I'll actually do this

The Fourth Amendment protects us from unreasonable search and seizure, but it was written before surveillance capitalism, before facial recognition technology, and before data brokers sold your location history to the highest bidder. Digital privacy is a civil rights issue. So is protection from a government that increasingly treats its own citizens as suspects.

  • Oppose warrantless government surveillance of US citizens.
  • Support the Fourth Amendment Is Not For Sale Act to ban law enforcement from purchasing data brokers' records they couldn't otherwise obtain without a warrant.
  • Support comprehensive federal data privacy legislation with real enforcement — not voluntary industry guidelines.
  • Oppose facial recognition technology in law enforcement without a warrant and independent oversight.
  • Support the right to know what data corporations collect about you and the right to have it deleted.
  • Oppose any federal digital ID system that enables mass surveillance.
  • Protect immigrant communities from warrantless surveillance and data sharing between federal agencies and local law enforcement. ICE should not be able to access DMV records, school data, or healthcare information without a warrant.

Endless war and unchecked power make us less safe. Strength means diplomacy, accountability, and restraint.

Deliver for the Central Coast
I grew up here. I live here. I'm fighting for the coast, the land, the farms, and the people who make this place worth fighting for.
How I'll actually do this

Calling our natural world a "natural resource" implies that it is an asset with financial value waiting to be extracted and monetized. This couldn't be further from the case.

Our public lands are also under direct threat. The Trump administration has moved to open federal lands to extraction, roll back monument protections, and sell off public assets that belong to all of us. I will fight that at every turn.

Climate change isn't a future problem on the Central Coast. Wildfires in the Los Padres. Drought across the region. Coastal erosion in Carpinteria and Ventura. The communities I grew up in are on the front lines, and federal policy needs to match that reality.

The Sable pipeline fight is happening in our backyard right now, and it is a prime example of what this campaign is about. The pipeline off our coast spilled thousands of barrels of crude oil in 2015 and has been shut down ever since. When state courts and regulators blocked its restart due to unresolved safety violations, the Trump administration invoked Cold War-era emergency powers under the Defense Production Act, a law never before used this way, to override California law, bypass a court injunction, and force the pipeline back online for a Texas oil company. A California court has since confirmed that Trump and his Big Oil donors are not above the law. This is not an energy policy dispute. It is executive overreach, contempt for court authority, and a betrayal of the Central Coast, all in service of an oil company that has accumulated criminal charges and a documented pattern of noncompliance. I will fight this in Congress using every tool available, including oversight hearings and opposing any federal funding that enables it.

Defending our coast and public lands:

  • Oppose the Sable pipeline restart until all state safety requirements and court-ordered conditions are fully met. No federal emergency power should be used to override a court injunction protecting our coast.
  • Oppose any use of the Defense Production Act to circumvent state environmental law or court orders.
  • Support California's lawsuit against the Trump administration's attempt to federalize the Sable pipeline and bypass state oversight.
  • Oppose any new offshore oil drilling leases along the California coast.
  • Defend the Chumash Heritage National Marine Sanctuary which was recently designated and already under threat.
  • Protect Channel Islands National Park and the surrounding marine sanctuary from offshore drilling and industrial development.
  • Push for coastal resilience funding specifically for Central Coast communities.
  • Oppose the sale or transfer of federal public lands. These lands belong to all Americans and must remain public.
  • Defend the Carrizo Plain National Monument and all monument designations in CA-24 against rollback or reduction.

Protecting our forests and water:

  • Support federal funding for wildfire prevention and forest management in the Los Padres National Forest. Its 1.75 million acres protect our water supply, wildlife, and communities.
  • Oppose any rollback of the Inflation Reduction Act's clean energy investments.

Building a clean energy future:

  • Support the clean energy transition as an economic opportunity including new jobs in solar, wind, and efficiency.
  • Support high speed rail investment. The California line runs through CA-24 and restoring the $4 billion in pulled federal funding is a priority.
  • Rejoin the Paris Agreement and restore the US commitment to international climate targets.
How I'll actually do this

CA-24 includes some of the most productive agricultural land in the world. The workers who farm it often can't afford to live here. The farmers who own it face water costs, consolidation pressure, and climate risk.

  • Support water infrastructure investment for drought resilience in SLO and Ventura counties.
  • Oppose agricultural consolidation that squeezes out family farms.
  • Push for farmworker housing funding specific to high cost-of-living agricultural regions.
  • Support the H-2A agricultural visa reform to protect workers from exploitation.
  • Invest in agricultural research partnerships with Cal Poly and UCSB.
How I'll actually do this

Your representative works for you. CA-24 spans Santa Barbara, San Luis Obispo, and Ventura counties: three distinct communities with different needs. I will invest in strong, accessible constituent services offices across the district and treat casework as a core function, not an afterthought.

That power belongs to every constituent in CA-24. I will staff my office to use it.

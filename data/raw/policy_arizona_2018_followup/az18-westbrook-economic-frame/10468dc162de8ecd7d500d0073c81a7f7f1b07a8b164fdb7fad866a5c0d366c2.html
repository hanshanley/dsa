Government’s War and Exploitation of the Poor & Middle-Class

It has long been known that the single boost to getting people out of poverty is education. From early childhood intervention to degree and certification programs, study after study shows us that this is the way to create individual and family prosperity.

An educated, productive populace makes a nation more competitive in the global marketplace so it is to society’s advantage to educate everyone.

If the government truly wants what is best for their citizens, their nation, and their future, it will be pro-education.

Disadvantaged and even middle-class students have a tough battle. Even if students have innate talent toward music, dance, or sports, their family financial situation may be such that they cannot afford to participate in these activities and never have an opportunity to develop their craft. Their homes are in less affluent neighborhoods so they live in poorer school districts. There are more families with both parents working so there are fewer adults who are volunteering in the classrooms. Academic failure may be modeled and poverty may be normalized.

In short, these students don’t have equal overall educational opportunities as their more fortunate peers. To add insult to this, blue collar jobs are disappearing at an astonishing rate so, without an education, their prospects will be bleaker. Without high-paying jobs, they are not contributing to the tax base.

Children from wealthy families tend to fare better because there are resources for tutors, treatment programs, health care, extracurricular activities, transportation, and especially decent food – something most of us take for granted. This is why so many government programs focus on feeding children a breakfast or giving them a free lunch – you can’t learn if you are hungry. Excelling outside of the classroom leads to scholarship programs, and networking with successful business leaders can open doors to coveted internships and first jobs.

This is what is known as “privilege.”

When it comes time for college and earning a degree — the single biggest predictor of one’s ability to climb out of poverty – poorer students will likely struggle to pay tuition, despite any financial assistance they may receive. Once graduated, they may not have the introductions that lead to easy first jobs, they likely will not own a car, and they will struggle to pay their student loans.

So, it would make sense that the government would level the playing field, right? Even if they don’t really care about the needs of the poor, they should care about our competitiveness as a nation and make education a right for everyone, correct?

If you support the Republican Party, you are creating obstacles to gaining an education. Understand that you are supporting a government that wants to take away food from children, cost families more for health care, and make it harder to pay back student loans. And they are doing this not because it lowers taxes for everyone and ultimately benefits you, but because it gives very big tax breaks to the wealthiest 1% and lines the coffers of corporations. This, in turn, means more money for the politicians’ campaigns.

The GOP is literally exploiting poor and working-class families to give more money to billionaires.

Make no mistake, this is war.

You are choosing a side and the very future of our nation with the candidates you support. If you are not supporting candidates who are pro-education, you are undermining our prosperity. Maybe you don’t care because this doesn’t affect you. Understand that if it does not now, it will very soon in the future. Because politicians who will exploit the poor will have no problem going after you. You can expect to be robbed of your own future, to have your rights trampled, to watch as the services you need are cut to give more money to greedy globalists.

It’s coming.

The question is when you will decide to vote against it.


I am running in 2018 and I hope you will support pro-education by voting for me.

Candidate First Name: Kevin

State Where Candidate is Running: Illinois
Congressional Chamber: U.S. House of Representatives
Congressional District: IL-08
Party Affiliation (optional): Democrat
Candidate Questions & Responses
1. Medicaid Cuts Under H.R. 1
I strongly oppose any legislation that increases the number of uninsured
Americans—especially when nearly 42% of people living with HIV in Illinois rely on
Medicaid for coverage. Health care is a human right. I would work to reverse harmful
Medicaid cuts and expand federal support for Medicaid, including strengthening enhanced
federal matching rates for states like Illinois. I support protecting the Affordable Care Act,
expanding eligibility, closing coverage gaps, and ensuring continuous coverage protections. I
would also advocate for targeted federal stabilization funds for safety-net hospitals and
Ryan White providers that serve communities most impacted by HIV. Protecting Medicaid is
both morally right and fiscally responsible.
2. Domestic HIV Program Funding Cuts
Yes. I unequivocally commit to opposing cuts to domestic HIV programs and to supporting
increased federal investment in prevention, treatment, research, and housing. Eliminating
CDC HIV Prevention programs and slashing the Ryan White Program would reverse decades
of progress. I support fully funding the Ending the HIV Epidemic Initiative, protecting the
Minority AIDS Initiative, and strengthening the Ryan White Program’s core medical and
supportive services. To end the epidemic, we must scale up testing, expand access to PrEP
and long-acting injectable prevention, reduce stigma, and invest in culturally competent
community-based providers.

3. Housing & Homelessness Policies
Housing is health care. Stable housing dramatically improves adherence to HIV treatment
and reduces transmission. I support reinstating and strengthening Housing First policies,
protecting HIV-dedicated housing units, and expanding rental assistance for people living
with HIV. I will advocate for increased funding for HOPWA and the Continuum of Care
program, and oppose policy changes that reduce permanent supportive housing.
4. HOPWA Program
Yes. HOPWA is essential to ensuring that people living with HIV can access stable housing. I
will defend and increase HOPWA funding, oppose harmful regulatory rollbacks, and push for
modernization to reflect current housing costs. Housing stability saves lives and reduces
long-term health care costs.
5. PrEP Access & National Program
Yes. I support establishing and funding a national PrEP program to eliminate cost barriers
for uninsured and underinsured individuals. Long-acting injectable PrEP represents a
breakthrough in prevention, but only if it is accessible. We must address racial disparities in
PrEP uptake by investing in culturally competent outreach, expanding Medicaid coverage,
and removing prior authorization barriers.
6. Minority AIDS Initiative & Health Equity
Yes. The Minority AIDS Initiative is critical to addressing racial disparities in HIV outcomes. I
will fight to increase funding and ensure resources flow directly to community-led
organizations serving disproportionately impacted communities. We must expand culturally
competent care and address social determinants of health including housing and income
stability.
7. NIH & HIV Research Funding
Yes. NIH-funded research has transformed HIV care and prevention. Cutting NIH funding
would stall progress toward new prevention tools and potential cures. I support fully
funding HIV/AIDS research and restoring eliminated research networks.
8. Harm Reduction & Overdose Prevention
Yes. Harm reduction saves lives. I support evidence-based approaches including syringe
service programs, overdose prevention centers, medication-assisted treatment, and

integrated HIV prevention services. Federal policy should empower states to adopt
life-saving public health strategies.
9. Protections for Transgender Individuals
Yes. I support passage of the Equality Act and broader federal protections to ensure
transgender and gender-diverse individuals are protected from discrimination in health
care, housing, and employment. Policies that marginalize transgender people undermine
our ability to end the HIV epidemic. As the first openly gay County Commissioner in Cook
County history, I understand the importance of representation, standing firmly for LGBTQ+
rights, and that is why I made gender identity a protected class for 5.2 million people..
10. Congressional HIV/AIDS Caucus
Yes. I will join the Congressional HIV/AIDS Caucus in my first year in office and actively
work to elevate the voices of people living with HIV. Policy must be informed by lived
experience and community partnership.

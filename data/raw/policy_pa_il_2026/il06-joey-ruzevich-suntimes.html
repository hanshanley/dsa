Joseph "Joey" Ruzevich - Candidate Questionnaire

Joseph "Joey" Ruzevich

Chicago | 29 years old

Candidate for U.S. House of Representatives, Illinois District 6
Democratic Party Primary

Occupation

Software Engineer and Small Business Owner who runs a mobile app development studio

Education

Computer Science & Mathematics Degree, Loyola University, New Orleans

Previous Political Experience

N/A

Campaign Website

Candidate questionnaire

To inform voters, the Chicago Sun-Times and WBEZ asked candidates to respond to questions about key issues. Answers are limited to 500 words per question and have been lightly edited for typos, minor grammar and consistency in styling, but not for content.

1

Economy

Grocery prices and inflation in general remain worrisomely high for most Americans. Please describe three steps that the U.S. should carry out to improve the economic outlook for Americans, both short-term and long-term.

Ruzevich's Response

Cost of living is one of the most pressing concerns affecting people in my district. The costs of housing, utilities, health care and groceries have skyrocketed due to a multitude of issues - one being consolidation of corporate power, which has led to widespread price gouging. We must break up monopolies in order to restore competition and keep down prices. Housing has quickly become one of the most unaffordable necessities in America. The anchor of the American Dream is completely out of reach for millions of people. Congress must take swift action to build new housing across our nation, prevent corporations and private equity firms from buying entire neighborhoods, and provide financial assistance to first-time homebuyers. It’s not just rising prices that are a problem. The federal minimum wage has been stuck at $7.25 since 2009. This is a poverty wage in every state in the country. We must raise the minimum wage to provide a living wage for all workers, and we need to index it to inflation. Then there is the cost of raising a family. We need to reinstate the extended child tax credit, which lifted 50% of children out of poverty and should never have been allowed to expire. Paid maternity leave should be written into law, with tax credits provided to companies to offset the maintained salaries. Congress must act now so millions of Americans are not priced out of having a family.

2

ICE Campaign

There have been major concerns in Chicago and surrounding suburbs about how the Trump administration has implemented its Operation Midway Blitz campaign. What is your view of the campaign and its execution, and what legislation would you pursue to get at the concerns you have about the ongoing operation?

Ruzevich's Response

The current actions of ICE are making our communities less safe, not more safe. I support the actions of local government bodies to restrict where ICE can operate (making areas like schools, places of worship and courthouses ICE-free zones), but the actions of ICE need to be curtailed at the federal level, as we have seen with how they have been able to operate with impunity due to the lack of jurisdiction and unwillingness of many state and local authorities to intervene. ICE’s racial and religious profiling has led them to target vulnerable communities in my district; not only undocumented immigrants but also legal residents and citizens. It’s unacceptable the way ICE is rounding people up, holding them in inhumane conditions and shipping people off to foreign prisons before any semblance of due process is provided. Students have been snatched by ICE for the “crime” of exercising their First Amendment rights to protest. ICE agents have even shot and killed several people then lied about what happened despite video evidence to the contrary. ICE has been operating with complete impunity and I will demand accountability through prosecution. I will fight for due process for immigrants. The targeting of law-abiding immigrants at school, workplaces and even when reporting dutifully as part of their immigration process is unacceptable. I will also fight for accountability to victims of ICE in cases where people were killed, assaulted, denied due process or improperly detained. I think about the quote on the Statue of Liberty often - one I’m sure my grandfather saw when he arrived as an immigrant on Ellis Island - that reads, “Give me your tired, your poor, your huddled masses yearning to breathe free…” This is the spirit of America I grew up believing in. We need complete and comprehensive immigration reform. For too long, Congress has been kicking the can down the road, leading us to the situation we have today where many do not have a viable pathway to become a citizen. They have created the problem at the border in part by making the legal immigration and refugee process so difficult. Additionally, Dreamers (DACA) were brought here as children and for many of them, this is the only country they have really known. We should expedite the citizenship process for them so they can become full citizens. There is a huge backlog of visa applications from several countries due to immigration quotas, leading to extensive waiting periods of a decade or more. We should continue to accept asylum seekers and refugees, but we need to address the shortage of qualified immigration judges so we can evaluate asylum claims promptly.
3

China

For the foreseeable future, a long-term foreign policy challenge for the U.S. will be China. What is your assessment of the Trump administration's policy toward China, particularly with respect to the imposition of tariffs and China's policy toward Taiwan, and what U.S. policy changes toward China would you advocate?"

Ruzevich's Response

We need to emphasize diplomacy over military dominance, and an ethnocentric approach will not work. We need to be respectful of the laws and culture of other nations, but we should also advocate for human rights at home and abroad. We need to strive to lessen tensions with China over Taiwan. What has happened recently with certain members of Congress advocating for military actions against China is unacceptable. We should be advocating for fair trade policies and peacekeeping. Tariffs should have to be approved by Congress. Tariffs can serve a purpose when implemented correctly, but Trump’s use of tariffs has been haphazard and based on personal grievances rather than what is best for the country. Trump didn’t make the investment required to increase manufacturing capacity in the U.S. for items he targeted with tariffs, including parts U.S. manufacturers needed to build their products. Other countries boycotted our products and formed trade alliances with other world powers instead. This was exacerbated by Trump’s imperialistic threats that are leading Canada and Europe to look elsewhere (including China) for allies and trading partners. As a result of Trump’s erratic implementation of tariffs, U.S. manufacturing declined precipitously in 2025. Farmers suffered major losses, and our citizens paid much higher prices for many products. The “revenues” he was collecting were basically taken from American consumers when they had to pay the extra costs of the tariffs. They are essentially a regressive sales tax. There are better ways to raise revenues. The whole economy was destabilized based on Trump’s day-to-day moods. Congress must act so the economy cannot be held hostage by one man again. We should strive to stay ahead of China technologically by investing in education, research and domestic manufacturing. We should lead the world in combating climate change and encourage international cooperation.

4

Democracy

There is increasing concern across the country about the erosion of democratic institutions, from the weaponization of the Justice Department for the pursuit of political enemies to mid-census redistricting to skew Congressional seats toward one party's favor. How concerned are you about these threats, and what do you believe should be done to firewall the country from those threats?

Ruzevich's Response

The Trump administration has acted with total disregard to the Constitution and the checks and balances of government. They are attacking the right to free speech and the right to protest. The President has basically declared war on cities throughout the country. ICE is grabbing random people and killing U.S citizens, with Trump shielding them from any consequences. Trump is using the presidency to enrich himself via real estate deals and cryptocurrency. He is also extorting the media, law firms, universities and other countries into doing his bidding or giving him bribes. Wealthy donors are having their personal agendas carried out to an extreme extent. He ordered extrajudicial killings on Venezuelan boat crews and forcibly removed the Venezuelan leader from office, openly stating he did it to get his hands on their oil, and declared he wanted to take over Greenland. Congress has abdicated its responsibility to pass legislation to keep Trump in check. When Democrats win back control of Congress, it is imperative we use that power to block Trump’s harmful initiatives and executive orders and protect people’s rights. That includes proactive legislation like codifying women’s reproductive rights into law, which should have been done years ago. Regardless of who is in power, members of Congress must use every tool at their disposal, including the bully pulpit, to fight back against abuses of power. Voters should choose their elected officials, not the other way around. We have seen the race to the bottom that began when Trump pressured the Texas legislature to redraw their maps, leading to both Democratic and Republican states around the country trying to redraw theirs to advantage their respective parties. None of this is good for the voters getting their needs met. Competitive districts are good for democracy. Without competition, elected officials become complacent and don’t need to listen to their constituents. The only way to fix the problem is to ban gerrymandering nationally or there will always be some states rigging the system. Independent map commissions with set criteria to draw logical, fair maps that ensure adequate representation would need to be set up in each state. Without action by Congress, we will not get this problem resolved, as the current iteration of the Supreme Court has given too much leeway to states to draw extremely partisan maps that disenfranchise many voters. We should pass the “For the People Act,” which bans gerrymandering but also addresses voter access, election integrity and security, campaign finance reform, ethics reform for all three branches of government and public financing of elections. This package of election reforms would help shift the focus of elected officials to their constituents rather than wealthy donors and corporations. I have personally pledged to refuse all corporate PAC donations. I will only be responsive to and representative of the people of the district, not the corporations that currently run our country.

5

Federal Funding

Cutbacks in federal funding have been used as a cudgel by the Trump administration against Chicago and Illinois, aggravating the strain on City Hall and Springfield finances. What if anything do you believe Congress could do to address this kind of action from the federal government?

Ruzevich's Response

Congress must draw attention to this inappropriate diversion of funds based on Trump’s personal grievances by using the power of the bully pulpit, whether Democrats have a majority in Congress or not. I am confident we will win back the U.S. House in 2026, and in that case we must pass legislation that restricts the power of the president so he may no longer withhold funding already allocated by Congress. The attorneys general from many states have been taking legal action, which is also essential to continue until this issue is resolved. Threatening to withhold funding unless states change their laws interferes with states' rights and is unconstitutional.

6

Local Problems

What is the most important issue in your district, and how would you address it legislatively?

Ruzevich's Response

My district is desperately struggling with cost of living increases rapidly outpacing wage increases. I already mentioned this in a previous answer, but I would like to reiterate this is happening by design. During COVID, we saw CEOs of public companies bragging about record profits while claiming price increases were due to inflation. This is called price gouging, and the federal government did nothing to step in to prevent Americans from being taken advantage of. Now algorithms set prices higher when a company believes you are able or willing to pay more for a product or service. Private Equity is buying up entire neighborhoods so families cannot compete in the market. This must be banned federally to give people a fighting chance at owning their own home, a core tenet of the American Dream. Health care premiums are seeing massive increases and draining the savings of Americans, and deductibles are so high many simply don’t seek medical care when they need it to avoid paying a bill they can’t afford. The federal government has the power to break up monopolies and prosecute price gouging companies. It has the power to outlaw algorithmic price fixing and corporations from buying single-family homes. Congress could take the burden of health care off the backs of working families by negotiating drug prices across the board and implementing a single-payer system like Medicare for All. The only reason none of this happens is our representatives being legally bribed via campaign donations by these exact industries to not pass this legislation. We need representatives who are of, by and for the community, who take no money from corporations and have a desire to deliver landmark legislation that positively impacts the daily lives of all Americans.

7

Final Pitch

Sum up why you believe you are the better candidate for this office.

Ruzevich's Response

My background is in software engineering, so I understand the technological challenges that so many in Congress do not. I also understand the potential threats to society and people’s livelihoods posed by issues like artificial intelligence, and I am determined to ensure that as technology advances we institute safeguards to ensure innovation is used for the good of society, not just to further enrich a few wealthy individuals. Sean Casten and I differ on several major issues. I support Medicare for All, where everyone in this country would have health coverage; my opponent does not. Casten is content with the status quo, where not everyone has access to health care and costs for everyone are steadily rising. Another major difference is that I recognize what has been happening in Gaza is a genocide and think we should withhold funding and weapons accordingly and ensure Palestinian self-determination. My opponent consistently refused to use any leverage to stop the killing of civilians and repeatedly said he did not think this was an important issue when Palestinians would try to talk with him about their concerns. Since this district has the largest population of Palestinians in the country, it is important they have representation. My opponent has not done much of anything to help constituents affected by the ICE raids, despite constituents begging him to help. In short, he is not responsive to the diverse communities within his district, and I am and would be much more responsive in office. As an elected official, I believe it is your responsibility to use your power to advocate for people in your district, especially marginalized communities. I don’t take any corporate PAC money and refuse money from AIPAC-affiliated organizations. My opponent takes corporate PAC money, including unethical companies and has accepted large campaign expenditures from Democratic Majority for Israel, a group that was formed to primary pro-Palestine Democrats. They ran misleading smear ads against his opponent, Marie Newman, in a previous race. I also am very focused on helping the working class, whereas my opponent seems primarily focused on wealthier constituents and corporate donors. With all the problems going on in our country right now, Casten focused on passing a bill loosening regulations on venture capitalists. This is all while constituents in his district are being rounded up and having their rights violated, and others are struggling to afford health care, housing and basic necessities. I think our constituents deserve a representative who will be focused on them. I am also younger than Casten and am not a career politician, but I think voters are eager to have younger people and those who are not part of the existing political establishment in Congress. So many Democrats in Congress are well past retirement age and have become out of touch with voters over the years. My campaign motto is “One of us - for All of us.” I am running to represent the needs of everyday people. I think many in Congress have lost sight of that.

    The Morning Call held an Allentown City Council candidate forum Thursday. Watch a replay.

    Share this:

    • Share on Facebook (Opens in new window) Facebook
    • Share on X (Opens in new window) X
    By Andrew Wagaman
    PUBLISHED: April 22, 2021 at 7:13 PM EDT | UPDATED: April 23, 2021 at 7:13 PM EDT
    Getting your Trinity Audio player ready...

    Allentown voters next month will largely determine who represents them at City Hall through 2025.

    They face many choices, but The Morning Call is here to help. Last week, reporter Andrew Wagaman interviewed all five Allentown mayoral candidates on why they believe they deserve to lead Pennsylvania’s third-largest city.

    This week, The Morning Call hosted and moderated an Allentown City Council candidate forum on Facebook. Nine Democrats and one Republican are vying for four open seats on the seven-member legislative body. The candidates discussed jobs, housing, taxes and policing, among other topics.

    Watch a replay above.

    Three Democratic incumbents face competition from six challengers in the May 18 primary. Republican Tom Houck will join the four Democratic nominees in the general election race.

    The three Democratic incumbents are Daryl Hendricks, Cynthia Mota and Ed Zucal. The six Democratic challengers are Tino Babayan, Santo Napoli, Patrick Palmer, Justan Parker Fields, Erik Rodriguez and Natalie Santos. All but Santos participated in Thursday’s forum.

    The Morning Call asked candidates to reflect on how the COVID-19 pandemic has influenced how they have, or would, approach the role of a local public servant. Candidates also discussed how they would spend $57.5 million in general relief money Allentown is receiving via the American Rescue Plan, and what steps council should take to ensure there’s enough recurring revenue sources to cover rising personnel costs.

    Candidates argued over police officers’ proper role responding to 911 calls involving mental health crises or substance use disorders, and whether some police funding should be reallocated to fund more social workers. They also debated whether the city should prioritize transitional housing, permanent supportive housing or preventive assistance in their effort to end chronic homelessness.

    The prospective councilmembers explained how they would streamline processes to start and run a small business in Allentown, and how they would provide more opportunities for youth and young families, among other topics.

    The primary election is May 18. Register to vote before May 4 here. Apply for a mail-in ballot here. Find your polling place here.
